After Lord Shiva severed Brahma's fifth head, something unexpected happened. Shiva's fierce form, Bhairava, had committed an act that carried a terrible consequence. /  Brahma was one of the divine creators, and killing him was considered the grave sin of Brahmahatya. As punishment, the sin itself took the form of a terrifying woman and began following Bhairava everywhere. /  Bhairava also became bound to Brahma's skull, which stuck to his hand. He could not get rid of it, no matter how hard he tried. /  Carrying Brahma's skull as a begging bowl, Lord Bhairava began wandering across the universe as a humble ascetic. He visited sacred places, seeking liberation from the burden of his sin. /  But the skull remained attached to his hand, and the spirit of Brahmahatya continued chasing him. Even the gods watched in wonder as the mighty Lord Shiva walked like a wandering beggar. /  Finally, Bhairava reached the holy city of Kashi, also known as Varanasi. The moment he entered the sacred land, Brahma's skull fell from his hand, and the terrible sin of Brahmahatya disappeared. /  The place where the skull fell became known as Kapala Mochana, the sacred place of liberation from the skull. /  Through this divine story, Kashi became associated with liberation, forgiveness, and freedom from even the greatest sins. /  Ending message:  This is the mysterious story of what happened after Shiva cut off Brahma's fifth head. It reminds us that even divine actions carry cosmic consequences, and that Kashi holds a special place in Hindu tradition.
