Why does the supreme playful god steal the simple garments of village maidens?

Deep in the forests of Vrindavan, young cowherd girls perform a sacred month-long vow. They bathe in the Yamuna at dawn, praying to Goddess Katyayani for a pure heart and a worthy companion.

Krishna watches them from the branches of a Kadamba tree. He decides to teach them a lesson about modesty, surrender, and the illusion of ownership.

Quietly, he gathers their sarees and climbs higher into the leafy canopy, leaving the maidens shivering in the cool morning waters.

Panic turns to laughter and awe as the girls emerge, finding the mischievous cowherd waiting with a gentle, knowing smile.

True purity is not about what we wear, but standing completely naked of ego before the divine.
