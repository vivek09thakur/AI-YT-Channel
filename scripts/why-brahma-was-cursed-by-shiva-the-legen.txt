According to Hindu mythology, Lord Brahma, the creator of the universe, once had five heads. /  One day, Brahma and Lord Vishnu began arguing about who was the most powerful among them. To settle their dispute, Lord Shiva appeared as an endless pillar of divine light, stretching beyond the heavens and deep into the earth. /  Shiva challenged Brahma and Vishnu to find the beginning and the end of this infinite pillar. Vishnu travelled downward in search of its end, while Brahma flew upward to find its top. /  After travelling for a long time, Vishnu accepted defeat and returned, admitting that he could not find the end. But Brahma, desperate to win, falsely claimed that he had reached the top. He even presented a Ketaki flower as false evidence. /  Lord Shiva immediately recognized Brahma's lie. Angered by his dishonesty and arrogance, Shiva manifested his fierce form, Bhairava, who severed Brahma's fifth head. /  Shiva then cursed Brahma that he would not be widely worshipped on Earth in temples, unlike Lord Vishnu and Lord Shiva. The Ketaki flower was also forbidden from being offered in Shiva worship. /  This is why Brahma has very few temples dedicated to him, while the Ketaki flower is traditionally avoided in the worship of Lord Shiva. /  Moral of the story: Even the creator must respect truth. Pride and dishonesty have consequences, no matter how powerful a being may be.
