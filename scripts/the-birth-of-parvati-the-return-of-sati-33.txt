The ashes of Sati grow cold, and the universe plunges into an endless, frozen winter of grief. Lord Shiva sits in deep, unmoving meditation upon Mount Kailash, lost in a sorrow that time cannot heal. The gods tremble, for without the divine balance of love and Shakti, creation itself begins to wither into dust.

High in the Himalayas, King Himavan and Queen Menavati pray for a divine blessing. They yearn for a child who carries the very light of the cosmos in her innocent eyes. Their devotion pierces the heavens, melting the hardest stone of despair.

From the ethereal realms, the essence of Goddess Sati stirs once more. She takes form not as a sudden illusion, but as a tiny, breathing infant cradled in the arms of the mountain queen. They name her Parvati, the daughter of the mountains, born with destiny written upon her tiny palms.

Even as a child, Parvati is unlike any other. She wanders through pine forests where flowers bloom instantly beneath her tiny bare feet. Animals of the wild gather around her, sensing the immense, slumbering power of the supreme mother resting within a mortal child.

As the seasons turn, little Parvati grows into a graceful maiden with eyes like deep forest pools. Deep inside her soul, an ancient memory awakens. She remembers a tiger-skin rug, a crescent moon, and a blue-throated ascetic lost in the snows.

One day, her father leads her to the hidden cave where Shiva meditates in absolute isolation. Parvati steps forward, holding a single lotus flower, ready to wake the universe from its long, frozen sleep. The air grows still, and the first spark of a legendary love dares to flicker in the shadow of Kailash.

Love is not merely born of the flesh; it is an eternal force that even death cannot conquer, waiting only for the right season to bloom anew.
