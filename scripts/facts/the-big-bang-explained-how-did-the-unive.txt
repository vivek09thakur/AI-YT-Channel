Have you ever wondered how the entire universe began? How did billions of galaxies, stars, planets, and even human beings come into existence?  The answer takes us back approximately 13.8 billion years, to an event known as the Big Bang.  But here's something surprising: the Big Bang wasn't an explosion in empty space. It was the rapid expansion of space itself from an extremely hot and dense early state.  In the beginning, the universe was incredibly hot, with energy and tiny particles filling the expanding space.  As the universe expanded, it gradually cooled down. Tiny particles began combining to form atoms, mainly hydrogen and helium.  Millions of years later, gravity pulled enormous clouds of gas together, giving birth to the first stars and galaxies.  Inside stars, nuclear fusion created heavier elements. When massive stars died and exploded as supernovae, they scattered these elements throughout space.  Over billions of years, these elements became the building blocks of new stars, planets, and eventually our Earth.  Around 4.5 billion years ago, our solar system formed. Gradually, Earth developed conditions that allowed life to emerge.  Simple life forms appeared billions of years ago. Through evolution over an incredibly long period, life became more complex, eventually leading to the incredible diversity of living organisms we see today, including humans.  And here's the most fascinating part: the carbon in your body, the oxygen you breathe, and the iron in your blood were created through cosmic processes involving ancient stars.  In a very real sense, we are made of stardust!  From the birth of the universe to the existence of human beings, it's a story spanning billions of years.  We are not separate from the universe. We are a part of it, and the universe lives within us!
