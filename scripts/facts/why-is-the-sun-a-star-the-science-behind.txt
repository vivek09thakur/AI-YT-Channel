Have you ever wondered why the Sun is called a star, even though it looks so different from the stars we see at night?  The answer is fascinating!  The Sun is actually a giant ball of extremely hot gases, mainly hydrogen and helium. Deep inside its core, a process called nuclear fusion takes place.  During nuclear fusion, hydrogen atoms combine to form helium, releasing enormous amounts of energy in the form of heat and light.  This is exactly what makes the Sun a star!  So why do other stars look like tiny dots in the night sky?  It's because they are extremely far away from Earth. In fact, the Sun is also a star, but it appears much bigger and brighter because it is the closest star to our planet.  Without the Sun, Earth would be dark, cold, and unable to support life as we know it.  So remember, the Sun isn't just a source of light. It's the nearest star to Earth and the heart of our solar system!  And that's why the Sun is a star!
