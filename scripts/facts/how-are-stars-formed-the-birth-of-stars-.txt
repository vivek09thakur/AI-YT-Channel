Have you ever wondered how stars are born in the vast darkness of space?  The story begins inside enormous clouds of gas and dust called nebulae.  These clouds contain mostly hydrogen gas, the main ingredient needed to create stars.  Over millions of years, gravity slowly pulls the gas and dust together, forming a dense and increasingly hot core.  As more material gathers, the temperature and pressure at the center continue to rise.  Eventually, the core becomes hot enough to trigger a powerful process called nuclear fusion.  Hydrogen atoms begin combining to form helium, releasing enormous amounts of energy in the form of heat and light.  And just like that, a new star is born!  Some stars shine for millions or even billions of years, depending on their size and mass.  Isn't it amazing that the stars lighting up our night sky were once just clouds of gas and dust?  Every star has a beginning, and it all starts with gravity!
