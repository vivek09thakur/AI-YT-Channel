Imagine a place in space where nothing can escape, not even light. Sounds impossible, right? But such a place really exists. It is called a Black Hole!  A black hole is an extremely powerful region in space where gravity is so strong that anything coming too close gets pulled inside.  But how are black holes formed?  When a massive star runs out of its nuclear fuel, it can no longer support itself against its own gravity. Its core collapses under its enormous weight.  If the remaining core is massive enough, it collapses into an incredibly dense region, creating a black hole.  But here's the most fascinating part.  A black hole has a boundary called the Event Horizon. Once anything crosses this boundary, it cannot escape, not even light.  That's why black holes appear completely dark.  But don't imagine them as giant cosmic vacuum cleaners. They don't randomly swallow everything in the universe. Objects can orbit around them, just like planets orbit the Sun, as long as they stay at a safe distance.  Scientists cannot see black holes directly, but they can detect them by observing how they affect nearby stars, gas, and light.  And here's something mind-blowing: some black holes are millions or even billions of times more massive than our Sun!  The universe is full of mysteries, and black holes are among its most fascinating secrets.  A place where time, space, and gravity behave in ways that challenge everything we know!
