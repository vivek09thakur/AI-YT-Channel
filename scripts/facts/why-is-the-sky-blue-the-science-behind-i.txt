Have you ever wondered why the sky looks blue instead of purple, green, or red? The answer lies in something called Rayleigh Scattering!  Sunlight may look white, but it is actually made up of different colours, each with a different wavelength.  When sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere, it collides with tiny gas molecules in the air.  These molecules scatter shorter wavelengths of light, like blue, much more strongly than longer wavelengths, like red.  That's why blue light spreads across the sky, making it appear blue to our eyes!  But here's something interesting…  During sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through more atmosphere. Much of the blue light gets scattered away, allowing more red and orange light to reach our eyes.  So, the next time you look at a blue sky, remember — you're witnessing the science of light in action!
