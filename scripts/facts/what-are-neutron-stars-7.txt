Have you ever wondered what happens when a massive star dies?  When a giant star runs out of fuel, it explodes in a spectacular supernova. But deep inside, its core can collapse into something incredibly small and unbelievably dense.  This is called a neutron star.  Imagine squeezing the mass of our entire Sun into a sphere just 20 kilometers wide. That's roughly the size of a city, yet it contains more matter than hundreds of thousands of Earths.  Neutron stars are so dense that a single teaspoon of their material could weigh billions of tons on Earth.  Some neutron stars spin incredibly fast, rotating hundreds of times every second. These are called pulsars. They shoot beams of radiation into space like cosmic lighthouses.  Even more fascinating, some neutron stars have magnetic fields trillions of times stronger than Earth's. These extreme objects are known as magnetars.  Neutron stars are among the strangest and most powerful objects in the universe, reminding us that space is far more mysterious than we can imagine.
